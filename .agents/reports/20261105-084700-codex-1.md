## Evidence and limits

- The oracle sources configuration once per process, then secrets in the same shell. A combined
  evaluator invoked again after startup repeats executable-configuration effects.
- A short-lived retained evaluator can preserve source count, unexported config state, literal
  runtime input, delayed secrets, and separate output sinks. It need not live through the runner.
- Actual runner secrets can call the real metadata writer, mutate dynamically scoped locals, and
  replace a function invoked by the subsequent runner. Returning selected settings and environment
  cannot reproduce those continuation effects in native Go.
- Auth command-substitution and direct-provider paths have different failure/output behavior;
  investigation `#162`'s universal source-failure claim is superseded by `#163`.
- Independent coordinator replay: `python3 /var/tmp/inv163/probe.py`, exit `0`, seven cases passed.
  Replay log: `/var/tmp/r06-inv163-verification.log`; raw results:
  `/var/tmp/inv163/cases-nceetzz3/results.json`. These use isolated homes and synthetic scripts on
  Bash `5.3.9`; they establish the reported counterexamples, not exhaustive or cross-version parity.
- The auth probes retain an existing cleanup-trap `tmp: unbound variable` diagnostic. This round
  neither repairs it nor treats it as evidence of generic source-failure semantics.
